Nvidia and the Risk of Depending on a Single Leader

Jean-François Lavigne November 9, 2026 3 min read

English version of a column originally published in French on November 9, 2026. Read the French original.

Jensen Huang has led Nvidia since its founding, more than 30 years ago.

He embodies the strategy, the technology, a significant share of the relationships with major customers and, to a large extent, the confidence the markets place in the company.

This week, the independent investigation announced by the board brought a simple question to the forefront:

What would happen if Jensen Huang had to leave Nvidia? (1)

The market has already reacted. Nvidia shares fell 4.3% on Monday after the investigation was announced. (2)

Succession alone does not explain that decline. But it illustrates something very concrete:

when a company depends heavily on one leader, whatever happens to that leader becomes a risk to the company.

No obvious heir

Nvidia has not publicly named a successor.

According to The Wall Street Journal, Huang has even been skeptical of succession planning. He also runs a notably flat organization, with roughly 60 direct reports. (3)

That does not mean Nvidia has no internal plan.

But it points to a very real risk:

a company can have an exceptional team and still depend heavily on a single leader.

A leadership continuity issue

Leadership continuity is precisely about reducing that risk.

Not by naming the next CEO today.

But by preparing what comes next.

Developing leaders who can take on more. Knowing the internal options. Keeping an eye on the executive talent market. Making sure the company can keep executing its strategy when a key role changes hands.

That is true for Nvidia.

It is just as true for a mid-sized company in Québec.

So the question is not only:

Who will replace our key leader?

It is rather:

What risk are we taking if this person is gone tomorrow?

And above all:

Have we prepared enough options to carry on without losing momentum?

That is what leadership continuity is about.

It does not begin when the leader leaves. It is built while that leader is still in the seat.

Sources
(1) NVIDIA, Statement from the NVIDIA Board of Directors, November 8, 2026. The board announced an independent investigation and reaffirmed its confidence in Jensen Huang and his leadership team.
(2) The Wall Street Journal, November 9, 2026. Nvidia shares fell 4.3% after the investigation was announced.
(3) The Wall Street Journal, November 9, 2026, “Jensen Huang Doesn’t Believe in Succession Planning. It’s Become a Big Risk for Nvidia.” The paper reports that no successor has been publicly named, that Huang has been skeptical of succession planning and that roughly 60 people report directly to him.
