Nvidia and the Cost of Dependency

Jean-François Lavigne November 12, 2026 2 min read

Follow-up to Nvidia and the Risk of Depending on a Single Leader, published November 9, 2026. English version of a column originally published in French. Read the French original.

Three days ago, I asked a question: what if Jensen Huang left Nvidia?

It just happened. (1)

Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress is now interim CEO. The board is searching for a successor, both internally and externally. (1)

One person, hundreds of billions

At the open, the stock fell from US$173.73 to US$148.80 within minutes. A 14% drop. Nearly US$600 billion in market value erased, if only temporarily. (2)

The products had not changed. Neither had the technology.

Only the person at the top had.

That is the cost of dependency.

The board takes charge

An interim CEO. Separate CEO and Chair roles. A search for a successor. (1)

These are sound decisions. But they are being made under pressure.

Prepare the options in advance

Leadership continuity is not about choosing the next CEO today.

It is about knowing where the risks are and preparing the options.

Who internally can take on more? What options exist in the market? How long can the company hold with an interim leader?

What about you?

Who does your company depend on?

If that person left tomorrow, would you have options?

Succession does not begin the day a key person leaves. It is prepared while they are still here.

Sources
(1) NVIDIA, “NVIDIA Announces CEO Transition,” press release and Form 8-K filed with the SEC, November 12, 2026. Read the release.
(2) StockTitan, “Stock Market Today Live, November 12, 2026,” and Share Talk, “Nvidia loses $888bn in market value as Jensen Huang resigns,” November 12, 2026.
